Pacific Plate

The Pacific Plate is the biggest tectonic plate on Earth, underlying most of the Pacific Ocean basin.

Its perimeter forms the famous Ring of Fire. As the plate drifts northwest at 8 to 10 centimeters a year, it dives under neighboring plates, carving ultra-deep ocean trenches, sparking major quakes, and feeding chains of volcanoes.

When this land bridge rose roughly 3 million years ago, it shut off water flow between the Atlantic and Pacific, permanently changing global ocean currents and climate.
