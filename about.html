About TectonicPlateMap.org

An independent science communication initiative and interactive 3D geophysics platform.

Who We Are

TectonicPlateMap.org is an independent science communication and Earth education initiative maintained by software engineer and geology enthusiast (@cbmgit).

We created this project to bridge the gap between heavy academic GIS software and everyday learners. Our goal is to make peer-reviewed geophysics research, real-time seismic monitoring, and continental drift dynamics intuitive, blazing fast, and freely accessible to students, teachers, and curious minds worldwide without paywalls, clunky software, or ads.

100% Open Source

We believe educational science tools should be transparent, verifiable, and free to examine. The entire frontend web client, MapLibre GL visualization engine, study guides, and quiz modules are open source under the MIT License.

View Source Code on GitHub

Found a bug, want to contribute a feature, or have geological data corrections? Check out our repository at github.com/cbmgit/tectonic-plates-web or open an issue.

Why we built this

Most plate tectonics maps on the web look like they were built twenty years ago, or they are locked behind clunky academic software that takes forever to load.

Earth's crust is moving right now underneath our feet, causing quakes and feeding active volcanoes every single day. We wanted to build something fast, beautiful, and completely open where anyone can spin the globe, click on any fault line, see plate drift speeds in real time, and learn how our planet works.

Where our data comes from

We rely on peer-reviewed research and official scientific agencies so the map is as accurate as possible:

Peter Bird (PB2002) Model

The plate boundaries, microplates, and drift speed vectors come from Dr. Peter Bird's 2002 benchmark geological study published in Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems.

USGS Real-Time Earthquake Feed

Live quakes are pulled straight from the United States Geological Survey API every minute. You can see exact magnitude, depth, and time of each tremor.

Smithsonian Volcanism Program

Our volcano catalog matches the Smithsonian Global Volcanism Program database, tracking volcano types, elevations, and recent eruption dates.

NOAA IBTrACS Storm Records

Historical Category 5 hurricane and typhoon tracks are pulled from NOAA archives to visualize ocean and atmospheric energy systems.

How it is built

We cared a lot about speed and not making a bloated mess:

  • WebGL Rendering: Powered by MapLibre GL so vector polygons and layers render at a smooth 60 frames per second on both phones and desktops.
  • Offline First: It works as an installable Progressive Web App. If your internet drops, you can still view the plates offline.
  • Zero Location Tracking: When you hit "Find My Plate", your coordinates stay strictly in your browser. Nothing gets sent to our servers.
  • Lazy Loading: Huge secondary datasets like historical storms and volcanoes load only when you select that mode, keeping the initial page weight tiny.
